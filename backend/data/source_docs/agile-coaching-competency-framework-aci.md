# The Agile Coaching Competency Framework (Agile Coaching Institute / ICAgile)

## 1. Foundational Architecture & Core Philosophy
Developed by **Lyssa Adkins** and **Michael Spayd** at the **Agile Coaching Institute (ACI)**—and subsequently adopted as the foundational standard for the **ICAgile Certified Professional in Agile Coaching (ICP-ACC)** and **ICAgile Certified Expert in Agile Coaching (ICE-AC)** tracks—the **Agile Coaching Competency Framework** (commonly known as the *X-Wing Model*) defines the complete capability spectrum required to guide individuals, teams, and enterprises through sustainable agile transformation.

* **Core Design Premise:** Effective agile coaching requires balancing **content-neutral process stances** (Facilitating, Professional Coaching) with **content-rich domain stances** (Teaching, Mentoring), anchored by **Agile-Lean Practitioner** credibility and supported by **three specialized mastery domains** (Technical, Business, and Transformation Mastery).
* **Self-Mastery as the Center of Gravity:** At the intersection of all competencies sits the coach’s own **Self-Mastery** (emotional intelligence, presence, awareness of personal bias, and non-attachment to outcomes). Without self-mastery, coaches default to rescuing teams or imposing rigid dogma.

---

## 2. The Core Foundation: Agile-Lean Practitioner
Before adopting any coaching stance, the practitioner must possess deep, lived fluency in Agile values, Lean thinking, and empirical delivery frameworks.

* **Scope of Capability:**
  * Fluent in the *Agile Manifesto* (4 Values, 12 Principles), Scrum, Kanban, Extreme Programming (XP), and Lean product development.
  * Understands not only the mechanics (*"How"* to run a Sprint Review or set WIP limits) but the underlying empirical and systems-thinking principles (*"Why"* those practices exist).
* **Operational Rule:** You cannot coach a system you do not understand, nor can you adapt practices safely to edge-case contexts without mastering their foundational intent first.

---

## 3. The Four Primary Coaching Stances (The X-Wing Blades)
A principal Agile Coach dynamically shifts between four distinct stances based on the team's maturity, psychological safety, urgency, and learning needs.

| Coaching Stance | Orientation | Primary Intent | When to Deploy | Failure Mode if Overused |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Teaching** | Content-Rich / Directive | Impart new knowledge, frameworks, or concepts clearly and concisely. | Team lacks foundational knowledge (e.g., new to Scrum, Kanban, or User Story Mapping). | Creates passive dependency; lectures teams instead of letting them experience problem-solving. |
| **Mentoring** | Content-Rich / Advisory | Share hard-won experience, patterns, and options while leaving the choice to the mentee. | Team understands the basics but faces nuanced execution challenges (e.g., PO struggling with stakeholder pushback). | Disguised consulting ("Here is what you must do") that erodes team ownership. |
| **Facilitating** | Content-Neutral / Process | Design and hold a structured, neutral container so a group can think, collaborate, and decide effectively. | High-stakes ceremonies, retrospectives, conflict resolution, or multi-team alignment workshops. | Over-engineering meetings or steering group outcomes subtly through biased questioning. |
| **Professional Coaching** | Content-Neutral / Inquiry | Partner with individuals or teams via powerful questioning to unlock their own insight and accountability. | Capable individuals/teams are stuck in limiting beliefs, blind spots, or interpersonal friction. | Asking endless Socratic questions during a genuine knowledge gap or production crisis. |

### Stance-Switching Execution Protocol
1. **Contract Explicitly:** State your stance transition out loud (*"I am stepping out of my neutral facilitator role for two minutes to put on my teacher hat and explain Cost of Delay..."*).
2. **Pull vs. Push Calibration:** Default to **Pull** stances (Facilitating, Professional Coaching) when capability exists; shift briefly to **Push** stances (Teaching, Mentoring) when a genuine capability gap blocks progress, then immediately step back.
3. **Shu-Ha-Ri Alignment:**
   * **Shu (Follow the Rule):** Heavy **Teaching** and **Facilitating** to establish foundational discipline.
   * **Ha (Break the Rule):** Shift toward **Mentoring** and **Professional Coaching** as teams experiment and adapt practices.
   * **Ri (Be the Rule):** Pure **Professional Coaching** and systemic **Transformation Mastery** as teams innovate autonomously.

## 4. The Three Mastery Domains (The Specialization Anchors)
Because no single human can master every enterprise discipline deeply, the ACI framework requires an Agile Coach to maintain working fluency across all three mastery domains while cultivating deep expertise in **at least one** (forming a "T-shaped" or "M-shaped" coach profile).

### 4.1 Technical Mastery
* **Focus:** Extreme Programming (XP) practices, Test-Driven Development (TDD), Behavior-Driven Development (BDD), Continuous Integration / Continuous Delivery (CI/CD), DevOps telemetry, evolutionary architecture, and technical debt remediation.
* **Impact on Agility:** Without Technical Mastery, business agility hits a hard ceiling—teams may plan in two-week Sprints but take six months to integrate, test, and deploy safely.
* **Coaching Application:** Pairing with developers on refactoring legacy monoliths, coaching engineering leads on trunk-based development, and helping Product Owners visualize technical debt economics.

### 4.2 Business Mastery
* **Focus:** Product management, Lean Startup experimentation, customer discovery, value stream economics, Cost of Delay, outcome-based roadmapping (OKRs), and business model innovation.
* **Impact on Agility:** Prevents "feature factory" dysfunction where engineering teams build the wrong product faster.
* **Coaching Application:** Coaching Product Owners and executives to shift from output-based Gantt plans to hypothesis-driven discovery, customer validation, and Evidence-Based Management (EBM).

### 4.3 Transformation Mastery
* **Focus:** Organizational change management (ADKAR, Kotter, Satir Change Model), systems thinking, leadership development, HR/Finance alignment (Beyond Budgeting), and enterprise culture evolution (Westrum / Schein).
* **Impact on Agility:** Addresses systemic organizational gravity (annual budgeting, individual performance appraisals, siloed functional handoffs) that otherwise crushes local team agility.
* **Coaching Application:** Facilitating executive leadership offsites, designing enterprise transformation roadmaps, and coaching C-suite leaders on intent-based leadership and psychological safety.

## 5. Coaching Arc & Engagement Lifecycle
Principal Agile Coaches structure their interventions across teams and leadership cohorts using a deliberate, empirical **Coaching Arc**:

1. **System Entry & Observation (Listen Before Acting):**
   * Observe ceremonies, value streams, and informal communication without interfering.
   * Conduct 1-on-1 listening tours to surface unspoken pain points, power dynamics, and organizational constraints.
2. **Co-Created Coaching Agreement (The Alliance):**
   * Establish explicit mutual expectations with the team and sponsors (scope, confidentiality boundaries, desired business outcomes, and exit criteria).
   * Clarify the ethical boundary: *Coaching observations belong to the coachee/team; aggregate systemic impediments belong to leadership.*
3. **Baseline & Backlog of Coaching Experiments:**
   * Co-create a prioritized **Transformation / Coaching Backlog** framed as falsifiable hypotheses (e.g., *"If we limit WIP to 2 items per column, our median cycle time will drop by 30% within 3 Sprints"*).
4. **Targeted Intervention & Stance Fluidity:**
   * Execute high-leverage interventions across the four stances and three mastery domains, pairing closely with internal Scrum Masters and leaders to build internal capability.
5. **Capability Transfer & Graceful Exit:**
   * Measure progress against internal capability independence—the ultimate success metric of an Agile Coach is making themselves redundant.

## 6. Ethical Boundaries & Anti-Patterns to Reject

* **The "Agile Police" Anti-Pattern:** Enforcing framework rules dogmatically (*"The Scrum Guide says you can't do X"*) rather than coaching teams to understand empirical trade-offs and own their process.
* **The Hero Rescuer Anti-Pattern:** Solving the team’s problems for them (writing their user stories, resolving their dependencies, or running every Daily Scrum) because it is faster than coaching them to build the muscle.
* **Confidentiality Breach / Management Spying:** Reporting individual team member performance or candid retrospective disclosures back to line managers, which instantly destroys psychological safety.
* **Uncontracted Stance Switching:** Offering unsolicited advice (Mentoring) when a coachee asked for thought partnership (Professional Coaching), or interrogating a novice team with Socratic questions when they simply need clear instruction (Teaching).
* **Local Optimization Blindness:** Coaching a single delivery team to shave two days off their Sprint cycle while ignoring a 90-day upstream portfolio approval bottleneck or a 60-day downstream CAB release gate.
