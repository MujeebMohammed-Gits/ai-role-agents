# Agile Coaching Matrix & Training Resources: Enterprise Capability, Curriculum & Assessment Architecture

## 1. Executive Mandate & Systemic Purpose
An **Agile Coaching Matrix & Training Architecture** provides an empirical, non-punitive capability progression and enablement system across team, multi-team (program/value stream), and enterprise executive layers. Rather than treating agile training as one-off certification events ("sheep-dip training"), a principal-level coaching matrix aligns **competency progression (Shu-Ha-Ri / Dreyfus Model)** with **contextual coaching stances**, **role-specific learning pathways**, and **observable behavioral outcomes**.

### Core Design Axioms
1. **Capability Over Certification:** Credentials validate baseline vocabulary; behavioral capability is evidenced exclusively through systemic outcomes, psychological safety, and empirical flow improvement.
2. **Pull-Based Just-in-Time Learning:** Training must be delivered immediately prior to practical application (within 72 hours of launching a team, adopting a practice, or restructuring a value stream) to prevent forgetting-curve decay.
3. **Multi-Dimensional Assessment:** Evaluate coaches across **Self-Mastery**, **Agile-Lean Execution**, **Facilitation & Coaching Stances**, and **Domain Mastery** (Technical, Business, or Organizational Transformation)—never on a single dimension.
4. **Systemic Coaching Ratio:** Maintain healthy coaching-to-team ratios calibrated to organizational maturity (typically 1:2 to 1:3 for nascent teams; 1:5 to 1:8 for mature teams; 1:1 leadership pairing for executives).

## 2. Multi-Dimensional Agile Coaching Competency Matrix

This matrix synthesizes the **Dreyfus Model of Skill Acquisition** with **Shu-Ha-Ri** progression across eight core coaching dimensions.

| Competency Dimension | Level 1: Foundation (Novice / Shu) | Level 2: Practitioner (Competent / Late Shu) | Level 3: Senior Coach (Proficient / Ha) | Level 4: Enterprise / Principal Coach (Expert / Ri) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Agile & Lean Foundations** | Understands Scrum, Kanban, and Agile Manifesto values; executes mechanics by the book. | Adapts Scrum and Kanban fluidly; explains the *why* behind empirical process control and flow metrics. | Synthesizes Lean, Systems Thinking, Theory of Constraints, and Cynefin to design bespoke team workflows. | Architects multi-team operating models (LeSS, Nexus, Team Topologies) and evolves organizational paradigms. |
| **2. Professional Coaching Stance** | Asks basic open questions; frequently slips into giving advice or solving problems for the team. | Holds clean coaching agreements; practices active listening (Level 2/3) and powerful questioning without judgment. | Navigates deep team conflict, limiting beliefs, and systemic blind spots; coaches leaders 1-on-1. | Catalyzes C-suite mindset shifts; coaches executive teams through high-stakes organizational politics and ambiguity. |
| **3. Facilitation & Group Dynamics** | Facilitates standard Scrum events within timeboxes using basic formats. | Designs custom workshops; manages dominant voices, groupthink, and remote/hybrid engagement. | Uses Liberating Structures, Open Space, and conflict-resolution protocols to align large, contentious groups. | Facilitates enterprise-wide Big Room Planning, Value Stream Mapping, and multi-day executive strategy offsites. |
| **4. Teaching & Mentoring** | Delivers standard slide-based training; mentors junior peers on basic ceremonies. | Creates experiential simulations and interactive labs; pairs with Scrum Masters and Product Owners on backlog craft. | Designs role-specific curricula; mentors senior coaches and builds internal Communities of Practice (CoPs). | Establishes enterprise Agile Academies, internal coach apprenticeship programs, and executive workshops. |
| **5. Technical Mastery (DevOps/XP)** | Understands basic CI/CD, automated testing, and technical debt concepts conceptually. | Helps teams visualize technical debt, enforce Definition of Done, and adopt basic pairing/mobbing. | Coaches engineering teams on TDD, BDD, Trunk-Based Development, DORA metrics, and evolutionary architecture. | Partners with CTO/VP Eng to modernize legacy monoliths, platform engineering, and deployment pipelines. |
| **6. Business & Product Mastery** | Understands user stories, acceptance criteria, and basic backlog prioritization. | Coaches POs on Story Mapping, vertical slicing (SPIDR), and stakeholder expectation management. | Introduces Continuous Discovery, Opportunity Solution Trees, Cost of Delay, and Evidence-Based Management (EBM). | Advises CPO/Business Unit leaders on Lean Portfolio Management, Beyond Budgeting, and outcome-based funding. |
| **7. Transformation & Change Mastery** | Recognizes organizational impediments and escalates them to management. | Maps local team dependencies; collaborates with middle management to remove workflow blockers. | Applies Kotter, ADKAR, and Lewin’s Force Field Analysis to guide multi-team transformation waves. | Designs enterprise transformation roadmaps, governance restructuring, and HR/incentive alignment with the C-suite. |
| **8. Self-Mastery & Emotional Intelligence** | Aware of personal triggers; maintains composure during routine team friction. | Demonstrates high empathy, neutrality, and self-regulation during heated retrospectives. | Models vulnerability and non-defensiveness; uses self-as-instrument to sense systemic emotional fields. | Sustains equanimity amid executive resistance; maintains strict ethical boundaries and continuous supervision. |

## 3. Contextual Coaching Stance & Intervention Matrix

Principal coaches select their intervention mode dynamically based on **team maturity**, **problem complexity (Cynefin)**, and **urgency**.

| Situational Context | Cynefin Domain | Primary Stance | Secondary Stance | Concrete Coaching Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Newly formed team lacking foundational knowledge** | Clear / Complicated | **Teaching** (70%) | **Facilitating** (30%) | Deliver interactive simulation (e.g., Kanban Pizza Game) and establish working agreements. |
| **Team executing mechanics mechanically without understanding purpose** | Complicated | **Mentoring** (50%) | **Professional Coaching** (50%) | Pair with Scrum Master/PO; ask "What problem is this ceremony solving for you right now?" |
| **Cross-functional team stuck in circular debate or competing priorities** | Complex | **Facilitating** (80%) | **Professional Coaching** (20%) | Deploy Liberating Structures (1-2-4-All, TRIZ, Ecocycle) to surface hidden assumptions and converge. |
| **Mature team facing a novel, ambiguous product or architectural challenge** | Complex | **Professional Coaching** (80%) | **Facilitating** (20%) | Hold space for hypothesis generation; guide safe-to-fail experiment design without offering solutions. |
| **Production outage, severe compliance breach, or interpersonal toxicity** | Chaotic | **Directive Intervention** (90%) | **Facilitating** (10%) | Stabilize safety/incident immediately; defer reflective coaching until system exits chaos. |
| **Executive leadership demanding velocity targets and fixed-scope dates** | Complex | **Teaching** (40%) | **Professional Coaching** (60%) | Illustrate queuing theory (Little's Law / Kingman's Formula) and reframe around Cost of Delay & EBM. |

## 4. Enterprise Agile Training & Curriculum Architecture

A structured, modular curriculum organized by **Organizational Tier**, **Target Audience**, **Core Learning Objectives**, and **Experiential Labs**.

### Tier 1: Organizational Foundations (All Employees)
* **Module 1.1: Agile & Lean Mindset Immersion (0.5 Day)**
  * *Target Audience:* All engineering, product, operations, legal, HR, and finance staff.
  * *Core Syllabus:* Empirical vs. predictive control, Agile Manifesto & 12 Principles, Lean waste elimination (Tim Woods), psychological safety, and outcome vs. output thinking.
  * *Experiential Lab:* *The Penny Game* or *Ball Point Game* (demonstrating batch-size reduction and cross-functional flow).

### Tier 2: Team-Level Role Tracks
* **Module 2.1: Empirical Scrum & Flow Mastery (2 Days)**
  * *Target Audience:* Developers, QA, Designers, Scrum Masters, Product Owners.
  * *Core Syllabus:* Scrum Guide 2020 accountabilities, events, and artifact commitments; Kanban flow metrics (WIP, Cycle Time, Work Item Age, Throughput); Definition of Done engineering.
  * *Experiential Lab:* *Lego Scrum Simulation* or *Featureban* (experiencing WIP limits, blockers, and Sprint Goal adaptation).
* **Module 2.2: Product Ownership & Value Engineering (2 Days)**
  * *Target Audience:* Product Owners, Product Managers, Business Analysts.
  * *Core Syllabus:* Product Goal & Vision, User Story Mapping, Vertical Slicing (SPIDR), Behavior-Driven Development (Given-When-Then), Cost of Delay / WSJF, and Evidence-Based Management (EBM).
  * *Experiential Lab:* *Story Mapping & Vertical Slicing Workshop* using an active enterprise domain epic.
* **Module 2.3: Scrum Master & Team Coaching Bootcamp (2 Days)**
  * *Target Audience:* Scrum Masters, Team Leads, Delivery Managers.
  * *Core Syllabus:* Servant leadership, coaching stances, Liberating Structures facilitation, Tuckman & Lencioni team dynamics, systemic impediment escalation, and probabilistic forecasting (Monte Carlo).
  * *Experiential Lab:* *Live Coaching Fishbowl* (real-time peer coaching practice with structured debriefs).

### Tier 3: Technical & Engineering Excellence Tracks
* **Module 3.1: Modern Engineering Practices — XP, TDD & CI/CD (3 Days)**
  * *Target Audience:* Software Engineers, Architects, DevOps/SREs.
  * *Core Syllabus:* Test-Driven Development (Red-Green-Refactor), Trunk-Based Development, Pair & Mob Programming, Evolutionary Architecture, Expand-and-Contract database migrations, and DORA metrics.
  * *Experiential Lab:* *Legacy Code Refactoring Dojo* (writing characterization tests and decoupling a tightly coupled module).
* **Module 3.2: DevOps, SRE & Platform Flow (2 Days)**
  * *Target Audience:* Platform Engineers, SREs, Infrastructure & Security teams.
  * *Core Syllabus:* SLIs/SLOs/Error Budgets, Shift-Left Security (DevSecOps), Infrastructure as Code, Golden Paths, and Blameless Postmortems.
  * *Experiential Lab:* *Chaos GameDay & Error Budget Policy Simulation*.

### Tier 4: Multi-Team & Leadership Tracks
* **Module 4.1: Scaling Agile & Socio-Technical Architecture (2 Days)**
  * *Target Audience:* Engineering Managers, Group PMs, Agile Coaches, Enterprise Architects.
  * *Core Syllabus:* Descaling before scaling (LeSS principles), Team Topologies (Stream-Aligned, Platform, Enabling, Complicated-Subsystem), Inverse Conway Maneuver, and cross-team dependency elimination.
  * *Experiential Lab:* *Value Stream & Team Topology Redesign Canvas*.
* **Module 4.2: Agile Leadership, Beyond Budgeting & Lean Portfolio Management (1 Day)**
  * *Target Audience:* C-Suite, VPs, Directors, PMO, Finance & HR Leaders.
  * *Core Syllabus:* Intent-Based Leadership (David Marquet), Ron Westrum’s Organizational Culture Typology, Beyond Budgeting (rolling forecasts, dynamic capacity funding over annual project budgets), and OKRs.
  * *Experiential Lab:* *Portfolio Kanban & Guardrail Funding Simulation*.

## 6. Coach Calibration, Growth & 360-Degree Assessment Protocol

### 6.1 Coaching Growth Wheel (Self & Peer Radar Assessment)
Coaches conduct quarterly self-assessments paired with peer/mentor calibration across **8 spokes** (scored 1–5):
1. **Agile & Lean Mastery**
2. **Professional Coaching**
3. **Facilitation**
4. **Teaching & Mentoring**
5. **Technical Craft**
6. **Product & Business Acumen**
7. **Organizational Change**
8. **Self-Mastery & Presence**

*Calibration Rule:* Scores are never tied to annual bonus payouts or punitive HR rankings. They exist solely to build individualized **Coach Development Backlogs**, pair complementary co-coaches (e.g., pairing a Technical Coach with an Org Change Coach), and guide Communities of Practice.

### 6.2 Internal Agile Coach Apprenticeship Pathway
1. **Shadowing Phase (Weeks 1–4):** Apprentice observes senior coach across team events, leadership 1-on-1s, and workshops; conducts structured post-session debriefs using the Coaching Stance Matrix.
2. **Co-Facilitation & Co-Coaching Phase (Weeks 5–12):** Apprentice designs and leads targeted segments while senior coach holds systemic observation and intervenes only if psychological safety is compromised.
3. **Solo Practice with Supervision (Months 4–6):** Apprentice leads 1–2 teams independently while participating in bi-weekly **Coaching Supervision** (reflective practice examining systemic blind spots and transference).

## 7. Operational Anti-Patterns & Failure Modes to Reject

* **The "Agile Police" Anti-Pattern:** Coaches auditing teams with compliance checklists ("Did you hold a 15-minute standup? Are story points estimated cleanly?") instead of coaching empirical outcomes and flow.
* **Certification Mills:** Measuring transformation progress by the percentage of staff holding two-day commercial certifications without measuring Cycle Time, Deployment Frequency, or Customer Satisfaction.
* **Permanent Dependency ("Crutch Coaching"):** Coaches permanently running retrospectives, updating Jira boards, and resolving team conflicts rather than building autonomous team capability and working themselves out of a job.
* **Coaching Without Sponsorship:** Attempting bottom-up team coaching while executive leadership maintains rigid annual fixed-scope budgets, individual performance rankings, and command-and-control governance.
* **One-Size-Fits-All Curriculum:** Forcing data science, platform infrastructure, and feature product teams through identical Scrum mechanics instead of tailoring workflows via Cynefin and Kanban.
